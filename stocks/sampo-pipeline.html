◆ The most predictive driver — three converging lenses

The master driver is the combined ratio — the underwriting margin. It sets the underwriting result and operating EPS, and the market rewards Sampo's discipline. Reported net income (swung by investment one-offs) is noise; capital return is the steady secondary.

Lens 1 — what moves the price (empirical)
Underwriting & operating EPS
The stock trades the underwriting result and operating EPS, and looks through reported noise. FY2025 reported EPS jumped 65% on a €540m one-off gain — but operating EPS grew 7%, and that's what mattered. Q1'26 even posted a −€46m reported net loss on investment one-offs while the underwriting result rose 9%. Weather and large claims drive the cautious tells.
Lens 2 — what moves fair value (computed)
The multiple, then the CR
On the earnings model (Module A), the P/E multiple is the biggest fair-value lever, with the combined ratio a clear second — together dwarfing premium growth and the investment result. And the multiple is itself the market's verdict on how durable that underwriting margin is.
Lens 3 — what management & analysts track
CR, RoTE, capital return
Sampo's medium-term targets are set on a group combined ratio below 86%, return on tangible equity above 20%, and >9%/yr operating-EPS growth, coupled with returning excess capital via dividends and buybacks. Analysts frame value around underwriting discipline, capital efficiency and dividend visibility.
Synthesis. The most predictive driver is the combined ratio (underwriting margin). It is upstream of the numbers the market actually prices: combined ratio → underwriting result → operating EPS → the earnings the multiple capitalizes — and Sampo's whole equity story is that its underwriting discipline (best-in-class ~84% CR, low expense ratio) is durable. Reported net income, distorted by investment marks and one-offs, is a distraction. Two things flank the master driver: capital return (a steady dividend plus continuous buybacks, funded by excess solvency) provides a floor, and the investment result (rates on the float) is a more volatile earnings lever. Watch the combined ratio and premium growth — not the headline profit line.

MetricFormula / definition
MC engine (Module A, re-used)Fair value per share = engine(driver draws) drawn from correlated triangulars with a one-factor copula and a regime-shock mixture. Exact formula per name is inside the pipeline's Module A footer.
Reverse-DCF (Module I)Solve engine(driver=x, all others at mode) = spot for x, by 80-step grid search over the driver's pipeline range. Reports x, the implied value at mode, and the % gap vs pipeline mode.
Forward P/E (Module I)Live spot ÷ the sourced consensus EPS estimate for the current fiscal year (≈ next 12 months) and the next fiscal year (≈ 24 months). Trailing P/E uses the reported TTM EPS. A non-positive EPS (loss year) renders as n/m.
Payoff-asymmetry ratio (Module I)(P(FV>price) × E[FV−price | FV>price]) ÷ (P(FV<price) × E[price−FV | FV<price]). Values > 1× = pay-off skew in your favour.
Win/loss ratio b (Module I)b = E[gain | FV>price] ÷ E[loss | FV<price], both in the stock's price currency.
Kelly f* (Module I)(b·p − q)/b, where p = P(FV>price), q = 1−p. Fraction of bank-roll to size if the MC is truth. Half-Kelly (or lower) is the institutional convention.
VaR & CVaR (Module I)VaRα = the α-quantile of (FV/price − 1) — the loss threshold. CVaRα = mean of the α% worst paths — Expected Shortfall.
Realised volatility (Module I)SD of daily log-returns × √252, over trailing 1y (252 sessions) and 3y (756 sessions).
Beta (Module I)OLS slope of daily stock log-returns on benchmark log-returns over trailing 252 sessions. R² reported alongside.
Max drawdown (Module I)mint(Pt/max(Ps≤t) − 1) over 5-year adjusted closes.
Momentum 1/3/6/12m (Module I)Simple return over trailing 21 / 63 / 126 / 252 sessions on adjusted close.
Own-history percentile (Module I)Fraction of trailing 5-year daily closes ≤ current spot.
Peer 12m relative return (Module I)Stock's 12m return minus peer's 12m return, both simple returns on adjusted close.
Return base rates (Module J)Empirical frequencies over the 5-year rolling windows: P(63-day return < −10%), P(252-day return < 0), P(252-day return > +15%).
Factor composite V/Q/M/L (Module J)V = 1 − price-percentile in own 5y history; Q = quality anchor from Module D (Piotroski / Altman / ROCE); M = logistic-scaled (12m − 1m) return; L = 1 − (1y realised vol / 45%). Simple mean × 100.

How the engine computes (Module A)

20,000 paths. Operating profit = insurance revenue × (1 − combined ratio/100) + investment result; fair value = that × (1 − 22% tax) × the P/E multiple, ÷ 2.65bn shares. Each driver is drawn from a triangular distribution: insurance revenue Tri(8.6, 9.0, 9.5 €bn), combined ratio (bounds 81–88%, your mode — drawn inverted so a stronger market means a lower ratio), investment result Tri(0.45, 0.65, 0.85 €bn), P/E multiple (bounds 11.5–19×, your mode). Dependence: a one-factor copula (loadings — combined ratio 0.70, multiple 0.65, revenue 0.55, investment 0.45, scaled by your correlation knob) plus a regime-shock mixture (your tail knob sets the shock probability). Correlation 0 and tail 0 reproduce the independent model. Tornado, scenario anchors and the Bayesian posterior are all computed live.

Sourced: fetched from a citable source (Sampo releases & IR, Simply Wall St / S&P, MarketScreener, Investing.com, GuruFocus). Computed: calculated here from sourced inputs (the simulation, forensic gauges, Sampo's own multiple).
