How the engine computes (Module A)

20,000 paths across five value legs. Marginals are triangular: Connectivity revenue 2030E $45–100bn (mode $70bn; 2025 actual $11.4bn, +48% y/y), Connectivity EBITDA margin 52–72% (mode 64%; 63% reported), EV/EBITDA 16–34× (mode 24×), Space revenue 2030E $8–30bn (mode $16bn; 2025 actual $4.09bn), Space EV/Sales 4–14× (mode 8×), Orbital-datacenter 2035E risk-adjusted revenue $4–120bn (mode $25bn, your orbital slider), Other-frontier 2035E risk-adjusted revenue $4–90bn (mode $25bn, your frontier slider), both on a shared frontier EV/Sales of 3–12× (mode 6×), AI-segment EV $250–1,600bn drawn around your central-mark slider. Fair value = [Conn·rev × margin × multiple + Space·rev × EV/S] ÷ 1.10⁴ + [(Orbital·rev + Frontier·rev) × EV/S] ÷ 1.10⁹ + AI value + net cash $25bn, × 1000 ÷ 13,170m shares. The two frontier-class legs are discounted a full nine years (2035E) and their revenue is already risk-adjusted (TAM × capture × probability — see Modules N and M), so each is priced as a deep call option, not a base case; splitting the old single frontier lump (mode $50bn) into orbital $25bn + other $25bn revalues nothing at mode. Dependence: a one-factor copula (loadings — AI mark 0.90, Orbital revenue 0.85, Frontier revenue 0.85, Conn revenue 0.80, Conn multiple 0.75, Frontier EV/S 0.70, Space revenue 0.60, margin 0.55, Space multiple 0.50, scaled by your correlation knob) plus a regime-shock mixture (your tail knob). Correlation = 0 and tail = 0 reproduce the independent model exactly. The loadings reflect relative sentiment-sensitivity — a modelling choice, not estimated coefficients.
