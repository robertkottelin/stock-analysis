Business thesis & macro place

What Micron is, where the cash comes from, and how it sits at the centre of the AI · robotics · data · energy build-out.
Sourced + judgment
BULL · THESISOf the names in this book, Micron is the most direct beneficiary of the AI build-out that still trades as a hardware cyclical. Memory is the co-processor to compute: every AI accelerator needs high-bandwidth memory (HBM), and Micron is one of only three vendors on earth who can make it. The thesis is strong; the valuation debate (Modules A–C) is entirely about how much of the super-cycle is through-cycle.

What the company is. Micron Technology (NASDAQ: MU) is one of the world’s largest makers of memory and storage semiconductors — DRAM (~70%+ of revenue: the working memory in servers, PCs, phones and cars) and NAND flash (the balance: SSDs and storage). It is the only US-headquartered scaled memory maker, against Korea’s Samsung and SK hynix. TTM revenue has reached ~$90bn with a ~76% EBITDA margin and net cash — figures unrecognisable against the FY2023 memory glut, when gross margin went negative.

Why memory is different now. For decades memory was a brutal, undifferentiated commodity: three players, boom-bust capex, margins to zero at the bottom. Two things changed. First, consolidation and capital discipline — DRAM is a rational three-firm oligopoly. Second, and decisively, HBM: the stacked, high-bandwidth DRAM bolted next to every AI GPU. HBM is capacity-constrained, sold out quarters ahead, priced at a large premium, and gross-margin-accretive. It has dragged the entire memory complex into an up-cycle with a genuinely new secular demand driver on top of the old cycle.

◆ Place in the macro stack — five trend lenses

Micron does not train models or design GPUs. It sells the memory layer without which none of them run. The question is which macro waves touch that layer — and here, unlike most industrials, the answer is “the biggest one, directly.”

1 · AI (direct, not narrative)
HBM for accelerators
Every AI training/inference system pairs compute with HBM. Micron is one of three HBM suppliers; capacity is pre-sold and margin-accretive. This is the core thesis — a direct, contracted AI revenue line, not a second-order tailwind.
2 · Computing & data centres
Content-per-server growth
Beyond HBM, AI servers carry far more standard DRAM and SSD content than traditional servers. Data-center is Micron’s fastest-growing end market — a volume + mix tailwind that compounds with every rack built.
3 · Robotics & edge AI
Memory at the edge
Autonomy, humanoids and industrial robots are memory-hungry at the edge (LPDDR, automotive-grade DRAM/NAND). Micron’s embedded/auto franchise is a long-dated call option on inference moving out of the datacenter.
4 · Data & storage
The data-gravity flywheel
AI both consumes and generates data at scale, lifting NAND/SSD demand across cloud and enterprise. A cyclical-but-secular support to the NAND half of the business, historically the weaker franchise.
5 · Energy & supply security
Fabs, power & CHIPS policy
Leading-edge memory fabs are enormous, power-hungry, and strategically prized; US/CHIPS-era incentives support Micron’s domestic capacity. Energy cost and grid access are real inputs — and a geopolitical moat for the only US memory major.
Investment thesis (one paragraph). Own Micron if you want the memory arm of the AI trade — a scaled, US-based, net-cash HBM/DRAM maker in a disciplined oligopoly, at a forward multiple (~5.6× forward EPS) that screams “the market thinks this is a peak.” Fade it if you believe memory always reverts — that HBM commoditises as three vendors scale, the DRAM cycle glut returns, and today’s ~76% EBITDA margin is a top, not a new plateau. The whole report is built around that single fault line: the durability of the through-cycle memory margin.

Read the peer bars as “the whole complex is optically cheap on peak numbers,” not as a discount to be arbitraged.

How the engine computes (Module A)

20,000 paths. Each driver is drawn from a triangular distribution: through-cycle EBITDA margin (bounds 44–80%, your mode), EV/EBITDA multiple (bounds 8.5–20×, your mode), revenue Tri($72, $100, $138 bn), net cash Tri($8, $20, $34 bn). Fair value = multiple × (revenue × EBITDA margin) + net cash, ÷ 1,129.4m shares. Dependence: a one-factor copula (loadings — margin 0.80, revenue 0.75, multiple 0.55, net cash 0.25, scaled by your correlation knob) plus a regime-shock mixture (your tail knob sets the glut probability). Correlation 0 and tail 0 reproduce the independent model. Tornado, scenario anchors and the Bayesian posterior are all computed live and mirror the Python engine in stocks/config_micron.py.

The three labels, precisely

Sourced: fetched from a citable source (Micron FY releases, the Yahoo fundamentals feed, sell-side consensus). Computed: calculated here from sourced inputs (the simulation, forensic ratios, Micron’s own multiples). Your assumption: an input you set (through-cycle margin, central multiple, correlation, tail, scenario priors, evidence weights, kill thresholds).
