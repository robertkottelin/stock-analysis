◆ The most predictive driver — three converging lenses

There is no single commodity spread. The stable core is grocery trade, but the most predictive mover of the share price is the building & technical trade recovery off its 2024 trough.

Lens 1 — what moves the price (empirical)
The construction cycle
Grocery is a defensive ballast that barely moves the stock. What swings Kesko is the building & technical trade division — comparable operating profit fell from €340m (2022) to a €116m trough (2024) as Finnish construction contracted, then recovered to €179m (2025). The tape tracks renovation/construction demand and rate expectations, not the grocery till.
Lens 2 — what moves fair value (computed)
B&T profit, then the grocery multiple
The tornado (Module B) ranks the building-trade operating profit as the single biggest lever on intrinsic value (±€6/share P10→P90), with the grocery EV/EBIT multiple just behind. Car trade and the two mature-margin drivers are second-order.
Lens 3 — what the anchor owner backs
Grocery share + trade turnaround
The K-Retailers' Association (largest holder by votes) and Kesko's own growth strategy name the value drivers as grocery market-share gains, the building & technical trade turnaround, and car-trade electrification — all routed through the same cyclical recovery.
Synthesis. The three reconcile on one variable: the building & technical trade recovery. Grocery (~€418m profit, 6.5% margin, #1/#2 in Finland) is the defensive floor and funds a ~4.6% dividend that pays you to wait; the swing — and the re-rating — lives in whether B&T profit climbs off its €116m trough toward the €280–340m it earned mid-cycle. As of Q1'26 the leading tell is constructive (growth resuming across all three divisions), and because the cycle sits near trough the payoff is right-skewed: more room up than down. The reverse-DCF (Module I) says the market is only paying for roughly the current ~€180m of B&T profit — not yet the recovery.

The one modelling choice, flagged. Divisions are valued on post-IFRS-16 comparable operating profit (EBIT) × an EV/EBIT multiple, and IFRS-16 lease liabilities (~€2.1bn) are excluded from net debt — they are matched by right-of-use assets and the rent already sits inside each division's EBIT via right-of-use depreciation, so the lease is not an extra claim on equity here. ◆ Every metric computed here — formulas

MetricFormula / definition
MC engine (Module A, re-used)Fair value per share = engine(driver draws) drawn from correlated triangulars with a one-factor copula and a regime-shock mixture. Exact formula per name is inside the pipeline's Module A footer.
Reverse-DCF (Module I)Solve engine(driver=x, all others at mode) = spot for x, by 80-step grid search over the driver's pipeline range. Reports x, the implied value at mode, and the % gap vs pipeline mode.
Payoff-asymmetry ratio (Module I)(P(FV>price) × E[FV−price | FV>price]) ÷ (P(FV<price) × E[price−FV | FV<price]). Values > 1× = pay-off skew in your favour.
Win/loss ratio b (Module I)b = E[gain | FV>price] ÷ E[loss | FV<price], both in the stock's price currency.
Kelly f* (Module I)(b·p − q)/b, where p = P(FV>price), q = 1−p. Fraction of bank-roll to size if the MC is truth. Half-Kelly (or lower) is the institutional convention.
VaR & CVaR (Module I)VaRα = the α-quantile of (FV/price − 1) — the loss threshold. CVaRα = mean of the α% worst paths — Expected Shortfall.
Realised volatility (Module I)SD of daily log-returns × √252, over trailing 1y (252 sessions) and 3y (756 sessions).
Beta (Module I)OLS slope of daily stock log-returns on benchmark log-returns over trailing 252 sessions. R² reported alongside.
Max drawdown (Module I)mint(Pt/max(Ps≤t) − 1) over 5-year adjusted closes.
Momentum 1/3/6/12m (Module I)Simple return over trailing 21 / 63 / 126 / 252 sessions on adjusted close.
Own-history percentile (Module I)Fraction of trailing 5-year daily closes ≤ current spot.
Peer 12m relative return (Module I)Stock's 12m return minus peer's 12m return, both simple returns on adjusted close.
Return base rates (Module J)Empirical frequencies over the 5-year rolling windows: P(63-day return < −10%), P(252-day return < 0), P(252-day return > +15%).

How the engine computes (Module A)

20,000 paths. Each driver is drawn from a triangular distribution: grocery comparable operating profit Tri(380, 418, 455 €m), grocery EV/EBIT multiple (bounds 12.5–18.0×, your mode), building & technical trade comparable operating profit (bounds 116–320 €m, your mode), B&T EV/EBIT multiple Tri(9.0, 12.0, 15.0×), car trade comparable operating profit Tri(65, 83, 100 €m) valued at a fixed 10×. Fair value = grocery + building + car − ~€300m capitalised group common costs − €1,308.9m net debt (ex-lease), ÷ 398.08m shares. Dependence: a one-factor copula (loadings — grocery profit 0.35, grocery multiple 0.45, building profit 0.80, building multiple 0.65, car 0.55, scaled by your correlation knob) plus a regime-shock mixture (your tail knob sets the downturn probability). Correlation 0 and tail 0 reproduce the independent model. Tornado, scenario anchors and the Bayesian posterior are all computed live.

The three labels, precisely

Sourced: fetched from a citable source (Kesko releases, Yahoo fundamentals-timeseries, ownership register, MarketScreener/Investing.com/Inderes). Computed: calculated here from sourced inputs (the simulation, forensic scores, EV/EBITDA). Your assumption: an input you set (building-trade profit, grocery multiple, correlation, tail, scenario priors, evidence weights, kill thresholds).

Still excluded — post-Modules-I&J audit

Still legitimately excluded — this build kept these out, not faked: The divisional EV/EBIT sum-of-the-parts is a transparent proxy, not a hidden number. Kesko discloses divisional comparable operating profit but not a divisional balance sheet, so each division is valued on its own EV/EBIT multiple and IFRS-16 lease liabilities (~€2.1bn) are excluded from net debt — they are matched by right-of-use assets and the rent already reduces divisional EBIT via right-of-use depreciation. Valuing on lease-inclusive EV/EBITDA instead would move the mix, not the conclusion. Beneish M-score — needs eight two-year ratios not fully disclosed; a half-estimated score is worse than none. Options skew, precise borrow cost, and detailed insider transaction flow — not reliably fetchable for a Nasdaq Helsinki name from a public API; would slot in from a Bloomberg terminal. Everything else previously flagged as "cut" (factor betas, own-history percentile, momentum, realised vol, drawdown, 12-month peer relative return, base-rate frequencies, owner-earnings yield vs real rate) is now included in Modules I & J and computed live from the Yahoo Finance v8 chart API.
